How the tool works

Left: what the patient sees. Right: what the tool does with it, as it happens. This is a walkthrough: nothing is recorded, and you choose what the patient "says".

The patient's screen

The steps

  1. The examiner fills in the patientAn ID code, age, years of schooling, and whether the screens speak to a man or a woman (it matters in Arabic).
  2. Ask permissionRecording starts only if the patient agrees.
  3. Check the soundThe patient confirms they can hear.
  4. Tell the story onceA hand-written story of about a minute and a half. One voice, the same for everyone.
  5. Record the retellingEverything remembered, in the patient's own words. Timed.
  6. Ask each question twiceFirst out loud: the tablet reads the question, then records the answer and times it. Then three options, each read aloud and lit up as it is read; the choice, the time and the finger path are saved.
  7. The examiner marks the recordingsAfter the patient has finished, so no right answer is ever shown to them.
  8. Count, and save one fileDetails: free recall, cued recall, recognition and the cue gain. Links: cued recall and recognition. The gap, the help from the options, the timings.
